## 1. Purpose

This procedure describes how departures from approved procedures, instructions, specifications or standards are reported, classified, investigated and closed at Acme Pharma Ltd. Its aim is to protect product quality and patient safety, to find the root cause of each deviation and to prevent recurrence through corrective and preventive action.

## 2. Scope

This SOP applies to all GMP activities at the site, including manufacturing, packaging, warehousing, engineering, utilities, Quality Control and computerised systems. It covers both unplanned deviations and planned deviations. Laboratory out-of-specification results are investigated first under SOP-QC-007. A deviation is raised under this SOP when the OOS investigation points to a cause outside the laboratory.

## 3. Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Any employee | Reports a deviation to their supervisor immediately on discovery, and records it in the electronic quality management system (eQMS) before the end of the shift. |
| Area supervisor | Takes immediate containment action and completes the initial deviation report. |
| Deviation owner | Is assigned by QA. Leads the investigation and root cause analysis and proposes CAPA. |
| Quality Assurance | Classifies the deviation, approves the investigation, decides on product impact and closes the record. |
| Head of QA | Approves all critical deviations and all deviation extensions beyond 60 days. |

## 4. Definitions

- **Deviation:** any departure from an approved instruction, procedure, specification or established standard.
- **Planned deviation:** a temporary, pre-approved departure from a procedure for a defined period or number of batches. A planned deviation must be approved by QA **before** execution. A permanent change is handled by change control under SOP-QA-012 instead.
- **Root cause:** the most fundamental reason for the deviation which, if removed, prevents recurrence.
- **Containment:** immediate action taken to limit the impact of a deviation, for example quarantining material or stopping a line.

## 5. Classification

### 5.1 Classification criteria

QA classifies each deviation within **2 working days** of it being reported, using a risk-based assessment in line with ICH Q9(R1).

| Class | Definition | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | Could directly affect patient safety or product quality, or indicates a serious failure of the quality system. | Wrong API dispensed; sterility assurance breach; data integrity breach affecting release data. |
| Major | Could affect product quality or GMP compliance, but has no direct patient safety impact. | In-process control outside limits; equipment used past its calibration due date; missing batch record entries. |
| Minor | Unlikely to affect product quality; a documentation or procedural lapse. | Late signature with a contemporaneous record available; a typographical error in a log. |

### 5.2 Escalation

A critical deviation is escalated to the Head of QA and the Site Director within **24 hours** of discovery. If a released batch may be affected, the recall assessment process is started.

## 6. Procedure

### 6.1 Reporting and immediate action

6.1.1 The person discovering the deviation stops the affected activity if continuing could affect quality, informs their supervisor, and records the deviation in the eQMS.

6.1.2 The supervisor takes containment action, such as labelling material "On hold", segregating affected batches or stopping equipment, and records it in the deviation report.

6.1.3 Affected batches are placed on QA hold until the deviation is closed or QA documents that release can proceed.

### 6.2 Investigation

6.2.1 The deviation owner investigates using a structured method proportionate to the class: a 5 Whys analysis for minor deviations, and a fishbone (Ishikawa) diagram or fault tree analysis for major and critical deviations.

6.2.2 The investigation reviews people, methods, materials, machines, measurement and environment, and checks for previous similar deviations in the last **24 months**.

6.2.3 Recurrence: if the same root cause has occurred **three or more times in 12 months**, the deviation is automatically classified one level higher, and a CAPA is mandatory.

6.2.4 Investigation timelines are:

| Class | Investigation completed within |
|---|---|
| Minor | 15 calendar days |
| Major | 30 calendar days |
| Critical | 30 calendar days, with an interim report to the Head of QA at day 15 |

6.2.5 Any extension needs a documented justification. Extensions beyond **60 calendar days** in total require approval by the Head of QA.

### 6.3 Product impact assessment

6.3.1 QA assesses the impact on the affected batch, other batches and released product. The assessment considers the specifications, stability data, validation status and regulatory filing.

6.3.2 Where the impact cannot be excluded, additional testing may be required. Any extra testing follows an approved protocol and is not used to test the batch into compliance.

### 6.4 CAPA

6.4.1 A CAPA is required for every critical and major deviation, and for minor deviations where a trend is identified. CAPAs are raised and managed under SOP-QA-008.

6.4.2 Where the CAPA requires a change to a validated process, facility, system or document, change control is raised under SOP-QA-012.

### 6.5 Closure

6.5.1 QA closes the deviation when the investigation is approved, the product impact decision is documented, and CAPAs are raised. CAPA implementation is tracked separately and does not delay closure.

6.5.2 A batch affected by an open critical deviation must not be released.

## 7. Trending and management review

QA trends deviations monthly by area, class and root-cause category. A summary is presented at the quarterly management review described in SOP-QA-008 and is included in each Product Quality Review under SOP-QA-015.

## 8. Records

The deviation record, investigation report, impact assessment and attachments are kept in the eQMS for at least **batch expiry plus 1 year, and no less than 6 years**.
