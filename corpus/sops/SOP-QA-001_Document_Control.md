## 1. Purpose

This procedure describes how GMP documents at Acme Pharma Ltd. are created, numbered, reviewed, approved, issued, revised, withdrawn and archived. It ensures that only current, approved versions of documents are available at the point of use, and that superseded versions are retained but cannot be used by mistake.

## 2. Scope

This SOP applies to all controlled GMP documents, whether paper or electronic, including:

- standard operating procedures (SOPs) and work instructions;
- specifications and analytical test methods;
- master batch records and master packaging records;
- validation master plans, protocols and reports;
- forms, logbooks and templates; and
- site master file and quality manual.

Records generated by executing these documents, such as completed batch records, are governed by the record retention rules in section 8.

## 3. Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Author | Drafts or revises the document and addresses review comments. |
| Reviewer | Checks technical accuracy. At least one reviewer must be from the department using the document. |
| QA approver | Approves the document for GMP compliance. QA approval is required for every GMP document. |
| Document Control | Assigns numbers and versions, manages the electronic document management system (EDMS), issues controlled copies and archives superseded versions. |
| Department head | Ensures staff are trained on new and revised documents before the effective date. |

## 4. Document numbering and versioning

### 4.1 Numbering

Documents are numbered as **TYPE-DEPT-NNN**, where TYPE is the document type (SOP, SPEC, MBR, FORM), DEPT is the owning department code and NNN is a sequential number. Department codes are: QA Quality Assurance, QC Quality Control, PR Production, VA Validation, IT Information Technology, EN Engineering, WH Warehouse. Numbers are never reused, even after a document is withdrawn.

### 4.2 Versioning

- A **major revision** changes the content or requirements and increases the first digit, for example 4.3 to 5.0. It requires training before the effective date.
- A **minor revision** is an editorial or clarifying change with no change to requirements, and increases the second digit, for example 5.0 to 5.1. It requires read-and-understand acknowledgement only.

## 5. Procedure

### 5.1 Creating and revising documents

5.1.1 Documents are written using the approved template, which contains the sections: purpose, scope, responsibilities, definitions, procedure, records, references and revision history.

5.1.2 Each revision includes a summary of changes in the revision history table. Where the revision results from a CAPA or change control, the record number is stated.

5.1.3 Any revision that changes a validated process or regulatory commitment requires an approved change control under SOP-QA-012 before the document is approved.

### 5.2 Review and approval

5.2.1 Review and approval are carried out electronically in the EDMS using electronic signatures. Each signature records the signer's full name, the date and time, and the meaning of the signature: authored, reviewed or approved.

5.2.2 The review cycle should be completed within **20 working days**.

### 5.3 Effective date and training

5.3.1 The effective date is set at least **10 working days** after approval for a major revision, to allow training. Minor revisions may become effective **5 working days** after approval.

5.3.2 Staff may not perform a task under a new or revised major-revision procedure until their training is recorded.

### 5.4 Issue and distribution

5.4.1 The EDMS is the master source. Printed copies used on the shop floor are issued by Document Control only, as controlled copies stamped "CONTROLLED COPY" in red with a copy number.

5.4.2 Uncontrolled printouts are watermarked "UNCONTROLLED — valid on day of printing only" and must not be used to perform GMP work.

5.4.3 When a revision becomes effective, Document Control retrieves and destroys all controlled copies of the previous version within **2 working days** and records the retrieval.

### 5.5 Periodic review

5.5.1 Every GMP document is reviewed at least every **3 years**, or earlier if required by a change, CAPA or regulatory update.

5.5.2 If no change is needed, the review is recorded and the review date is updated without changing the version.

5.5.3 Documents overdue for periodic review by more than 90 days are reported to the Head of QA.

### 5.6 Superseding and withdrawal

5.6.1 When a new version becomes effective, the previous version is automatically marked **Superseded** in the EDMS. It remains readable by QA and auditors but cannot be printed as a controlled copy.

5.6.2 A document that is no longer needed is **Withdrawn**. Withdrawal requires a documented reason and QA approval.

5.6.3 Superseded and withdrawn documents are never deleted. They are archived in the EDMS with their full approval history.

## 6. Good documentation practice

6.1 GMP entries are made in indelible blue or black ink, at the time the activity is performed.

6.2 Corrections are made with a single line through the original entry so that it remains legible, with the correct value, initials, date and a reason where the reason is not obvious. Correction fluid, overwriting and erasing are prohibited.

6.3 Data recording follows ALCOA+ principles: attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original and accurate, plus complete, consistent, enduring and available.

## 7. Electronic records

Electronic documents and records in the EDMS are protected by role-based access (SOP-IT-006), an audit trail of all creations, changes and deletions (SOP-IT-003), and daily backups.

## 8. Retention

| Record | Retention |
|---|---|
| Approved SOPs and their superseded versions | Permanent |
| Executed batch records | Batch expiry plus 1 year, minimum 6 years |
| Training records | Employment period plus 6 years |
| Validation documents | Life of the system or process plus 6 years |
