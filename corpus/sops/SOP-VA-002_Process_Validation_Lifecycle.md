## 1. Purpose

This procedure describes the lifecycle approach to process validation at Acme Pharma Ltd. It follows the three-stage model of process design, process qualification and continued process verification. The goal is to provide documented evidence that each commercial manufacturing process consistently produces product meeting its quality attributes.

## 2. Scope

This SOP applies to the validation of all commercial manufacturing processes for APIs and finished products at the site, including new products, transfers from other sites and significant process changes. Cleaning validation, analytical method validation and computerised system validation are covered by separate procedures.

## 3. Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |

| Validation | Writes the validation plan, protocols and reports and coordinates execution. |

## 4. Definitions

- **Critical quality attribute (CQA):** a property or characteristic that should be within an appropriate limit to ensure the desired product quality.
- **Critical process parameter (CPP):** a process parameter whose variability has an impact on a critical quality attribute.
- **Process performance qualification (PPQ):** the stage that combines the actual facility, utilities, equipment and trained personnel with the commercial process to show that the process performs as expected.
- **Continued process verification (CPV):** the ongoing collection and analysis of process data to confirm that the process remains in a state of control.

## 5. Procedure

### 5.1 Stage 1 — Process design

5.1.1 The process design stage defines the commercial process based on development and scale-up knowledge.

5.1.2 The output of Stage 1 is a **control strategy document** that lists the CQAs, the CPPs and their proven acceptable ranges, the in-process controls and the specifications. A risk assessment, such as an FMEA following ICH Q9(R1), is used to identify which parameters are critical.

5.1.3 The control strategy is approved by QA before Stage 2 starts.

### 5.2 Stage 2 — Process qualification

5.2.1 **Prerequisites.** Before PPQ, the facility, utilities and equipment must be qualified (installation, operational and performance qualification), analytical methods must be validated, and personnel must be trained.

5.2.2 **PPQ protocol.** The PPQ protocol is approved by QA before the first PPQ batch. It specifies the manufacturing conditions, the CPPs and their ranges, the sampling plan, the tests and the acceptance criteria, and the statistical methods to be used.

5.2.3 **Number of batches.** Acme's default is **three consecutive commercial-scale PPQ batches**. A different number may be used if justified by a documented risk assessment based on process knowledge and variability. The justification is included in the protocol.

5.2.4 **Enhanced sampling.** PPQ batches have more sampling than routine batches. For blend uniformity, at least **10 locations** in the blender are sampled, with **3 replicates** per location.

5.2.5 **Acceptance.** All batches must meet the protocol acceptance criteria. A failure is investigated as a deviation under SOP-QA-005, and the PPQ cannot be concluded until the investigation is complete.

5.2.6 **PPQ report.** The report summarises the data, discusses any deviations and states clearly whether the process is validated. The report is approved by QA before the commercial distribution of any batch from the process, except under concurrent release (section 5.3).

### 5.3 Concurrent release

5.3.1 Concurrent release means releasing a PPQ batch for distribution before all PPQ batches are completed.

5.3.2 Concurrent release is allowed only in exceptional circumstances, for example a limited-supply medicine with medical need. It requires a written justification approved by the Head of QA, and each batch must meet all release criteria and the protocol acceptance criteria for that batch.

### 5.4 Stage 3 — Continued process verification

5.4.1 After the PPQ report is approved, the process enters continued process verification.

5.4.2 For the first **25 commercial batches** after PPQ, CPPs and CQAs are monitored with enhanced frequency and trended with control charts. After that, monitoring continues at the routine frequency defined in the CPV plan.

5.4.3 Statistical trend rules, such as a point outside the control limits, or **seven consecutive points** on the same side of the mean, trigger an investigation.

5.4.4 CPV results are summarised quarterly and reported in the Product Quality Review under SOP-QA-015.

### 5.5 Revalidation triggers

A process is evaluated for revalidation when there is:

- a major or critical change to the process, equipment, materials or site, assessed under SOP-QA-012;
- an adverse trend in CPV data that cannot be explained; or
- a PQR conclusion that the process is not in a state of control.

## 6. Records

The validation plan, control strategy, protocols, reports and CPV data are kept for the life of the product plus **6 years**.
