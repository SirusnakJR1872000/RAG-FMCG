## 1. Purpose

This procedure defines how Acme Pharma Ltd. investigates, documents and closes any analytical result that falls outside an approved specification or acceptance criterion. It ensures that every out-of-specification (OOS) result is investigated promptly, that the root cause is identified where possible, and that no batch is released on the basis of an invalidated result without a scientifically justified and documented reason.

## 2. Scope

This SOP applies to all OOS results generated by the Quality Control (QC) laboratories for raw materials, packaging materials, intermediates, active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs), finished products and stability samples. It also applies to results produced by contract laboratories on behalf of Acme. Out-of-trend (OOT) results and environmental monitoring excursions are handled under SOP-QC-011 and are outside the scope of this procedure, unless an OOT result also breaches a specification limit.

## 3. Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Analyst | Reports a suspected OOS result to the laboratory supervisor immediately, and in any case before the end of the working shift. Retains all solutions, samples and preparations. Must not discard or re-test anything before Phase I starts. |
| Laboratory supervisor | Opens the OOS record, performs the Phase I laboratory investigation together with the analyst, and decides whether an assignable laboratory error exists. |
| QC manager | Approves the conclusion of Phase I and any retest or resampling plan. |
| Quality Assurance (QA) | Is notified within one working day of every OOS record. Leads Phase II, approves the final investigation report and decides on batch disposition. |
| Production | Supports Phase II with batch records, equipment logs and process data. |

## 4. Definitions

- **OOS result:** a test result that falls outside the specification or acceptance criteria established in an approved document, such as a registered specification, a pharmacopoeial monograph or an internal release specification.
- **Assignable cause:** a documented, demonstrable error that explains the OOS result, for example an incorrect dilution, a wrong standard, an instrument malfunction or a calculation error.
- **Retest:** a repeat analysis of the original sample, or of a portion of the original homogeneous sample, performed only after an approved plan.
- **Resample:** a new sample taken from the original batch. Resampling is permitted only when the original sample is shown to be unrepresentative or compromised.
- **Reportable result:** the final analytical result, as defined in the test method, used to make a batch disposition decision.

## 5. Procedure

### 5.1 Reporting and record opening

5.1.1 When an analyst obtains a result outside specification, the analyst stops the analysis, keeps all sample preparations, standards, glassware and instrument settings intact, and informs the laboratory supervisor at once.

5.1.2 The supervisor opens an OOS record in the laboratory information management system (LIMS) on the same day. The record receives a unique number in the format OOS-YYYY-NNNN.

5.1.3 QA is notified through the LIMS workflow within one working day of the record being opened.

5.1.4 The affected batch is placed on QA hold. It must not be released, shipped or used in further manufacture until the OOS record is closed.

### 5.2 Phase I — laboratory investigation

5.2.1 Phase I must be completed within **5 working days** of the OOS record being opened. An extension requires written justification approved by the QC manager.

5.2.2 The supervisor and analyst review, using the Phase I checklist (Form QC-007-F1):

- the calculations and data transcription;
- the correct use of the method version, standards and reagents, including expiry dates;
- the instrument calibration status, system suitability results and the instrument audit trail for the run (see SOP-IT-003);
- the sample preparation, dilutions and solution stability;
- the analyst's training record for the method.

5.2.3 If an assignable laboratory error is clearly identified and documented, the original result is invalidated. The test is repeated by a second analyst where possible, and the repeat result becomes the reportable result. The error is recorded and trended.

5.2.4 Invalidating a result without a documented assignable cause is **not permitted**. "Analyst error" without supporting evidence is not an acceptable conclusion.

5.2.5 If no assignable laboratory error is found, the OOS result is considered confirmed at Phase I, and the investigation moves to Phase II.

### 5.3 Retesting rules

5.3.1 Retesting is permitted only under a written plan approved by the QC manager and QA before the retest starts.

5.3.2 The plan states the number of retests in advance. For a single-value assay, Acme's default is **three retests by a second analyst**. Testing into compliance, meaning repeated testing until a passing result is obtained, is prohibited.

5.3.3 All retest results are reported. An individual passing retest result cannot override a confirmed OOS result without a scientific justification documented in the investigation.

5.3.4 Averaging may be used only where the test method specifies averaging. Averaging must never be used to hide an individual OOS value in a content uniformity or dissolution test.

### 5.4 Phase II — full-scale investigation

5.4.1 QA leads Phase II and involves Production, Engineering and other functions as needed.

5.4.2 Phase II reviews the manufacturing process, batch record, equipment, materials and environmental data for the batch, and checks whether other batches made with the same materials, equipment or process may be affected.

5.4.3 Phase II must be completed within **30 calendar days** of the OOS record being opened. If more time is needed, an interim report is issued at day 30 and the extension is approved by the Head of QA.

5.4.4 Where the investigation identifies a manufacturing or process cause, a deviation is raised under SOP-QA-005, Deviation Management, and linked to the OOS record.

### 5.5 Investigation and impact assessment

5.5.1 The investigation report documents the root cause, or, where no root cause is found, the most probable cause and the evidence considered.

5.5.2 The impact assessment covers the affected batch, other batches that share the cause, released batches already on the market, and stability commitments.

5.5.3 The laboratory supervisor shall initiate a documented laboratory investigation for every OOS result. QA confirms in the final report that the scope of the impact assessment is adequate.

### 5.6 Corrective and preventive actions

5.6.1 A CAPA shall be raised under SOP-QA-008, CAPA Management, when the investigation:

- identifies a confirmed assignable cause in the laboratory or the process;
- shows that the same or a similar OOS has occurred more than once in **12 months**; or
- reveals a systemic quality risk, such as an unsuitable method or specification.

5.6.2 The CAPA number is recorded in the OOS report.

### 5.7 Batch disposition and closure

5.7.1 QA decides on batch disposition based on the completed investigation. A batch with a confirmed OOS result is rejected unless a documented scientific justification, approved by the Head of QA, shows that the batch meets all quality requirements.

5.7.2 The OOS record is closed only when the investigation report is approved by QA and all immediate actions are complete. Open CAPAs do not prevent closure if they are tracked under SOP-QA-008.

5.7.3 OOS results confirmed for a released batch are escalated to the Head of QA within **24 hours** for a recall assessment.

## 6. Trending

QC compiles OOS results every quarter by product, test and laboratory. Trends are reviewed at the quarterly quality review meeting and included in the Product Quality Review under SOP-QA-015.
