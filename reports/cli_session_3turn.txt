You> What must happen after an out-of-specification result?

According to [1] ACME-SOP-QC-007 §1 Purpose (v3.0), the procedure defines how Acme Pharma Ltd. investigates, documents and closes any analytical result that falls outside an approved specification or acceptance criterion.

Additionally, [2] ACME-SOP-QC-007 §8 References (v3.0) references ICH Q7, section 11.15, which states that out-of-specification results should be investigated and documented according to a procedure.

Furthermore, [4] ICH-Q7 §11.15 General Controls (Step 4, 10 Nov 2000), p. 28 also states that any out-of-specification result obtained should be investigated and documented according to a procedure.

Therefore, after an out-of-specification result, the following must happen:

* Investigation of the root cause
* Documentation of the investigation
* Analysis of the data
* Assessment of whether a significant problem exists
* Allocation of tasks for corrective actions
* Conclusions

This is in accordance with [4] ICH-Q7 §11.15 General Controls (Step 4, 10 Nov 2000), p. 28.

You> And when does a CAPA have to be raised in that case?

According to [1] ICH Q7, section 11.1 General Controls, paragraph 11.15, out-of-specification results should be investigated and documented according to a procedure. However, the sources do not specify when a CAPA is required after an investigation.

However, [2] ACME-SOP-QC-007 §5.6.1 states that a CAPA shall be raised under SOP-QA-008, CAPA Management, when:

* The investigation identifies a confirmed assignable cause in the laboratory or the process;
* The same or a similar OOS has occurred more than once in 12 months; or
* A systemic quality risk is revealed, such as an unsuitable method or specification.

Therefore, based on [2], a CAPA is required after an out-of-specification result has been investigated and documented when one of the above conditions is met.
